# Exhaust (Ironclad)

**Goal:** Deliberately remove cards from combat (Exhaust) to thin bad draws out of the
fight and trigger payoffs that reward a shrinking hand. Possibly the highest skill
ceiling in the Ironclad kit — new players often see "this card disappears after one
play" as purely a downside, but a well-built Exhaust deck turns thinning into an engine.

**Early priority cards:**
- Burning Pact — the best early thinning tool: removes a card of your choice such as curses or statuses and
  draws two cards.
- True Grit — takes some risk when not upgraded due to its random element, but this is overcome once upgraded.
- Evil Eye, Forgotten Ritual — uncommon payoff cards (16 Block / 3 Energy respectively)
  for playing Exhaust cards; not guaranteed early, but auto-includes once seen.

**Consistency and power cards:**
- Ashen Strike — already fine as a plain Strike, and becomes a real finisher as your
  Exhaust pile grows in long fights.
- Feel No Pain, Dark Embrace — immediate value every time you remove a card; obvious
  includes in any Exhaust shell.
- Corruption — extremely strong but risky (an Ancient-tier reward): lets you play all
  Skills for 0 Energy while exhausting them, refilling your hand with Attacks on
  reshuffle. Requires careful sequencing, but in most fights you'll win before it costs you.
- Fiend Fire, Second Wind — higher learning curve. Fiend Fire is usually a finisher (though
  it can be played early alongside Ashen Strike); Second Wind rescues you from a hand
  full of bad status cards late in a run and can be played multiple times per fight.

**Key relics:**
- Joss Paper — draw a card every 5th Exhaust trigger; pairs naturally with this deck.
- Charon's Ashes — 3 damage to ALL enemies whenever you Exhaust a card.

**Playstyle notes:** Removing cards is one of the most underused tools in the game for
new players — thinning your deck to reach your combo pieces faster is a large part of
what makes this build work. Expect this line to come online more slowly than Vulnerable
or Body Slam, but it rewards patience with some of the sharpest turns in the class.
