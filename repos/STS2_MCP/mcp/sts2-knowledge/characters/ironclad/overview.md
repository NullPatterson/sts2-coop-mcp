# Ironclad — Overview

**Core loop:** Strength, Exhaust, and reliable sustain.
**Starter relic:** Burning Blood — heal 6 HP at the end of combat.
**Starter deck:** Strike/Defend package plus Bash (deal 8 damage, apply 2 Vulnerable).

## Playstyle summary

Ironclad is the roster's forgiving, fundamentals-first class. It rewards a clean damage
plan and a clean block plan over clever synergy, and it punishes decks that lean on
expensive payoffs before the defensive backbone is in place. It is the best class for
reading enemy pacing and elite pressure without heavy deck-specific setup.

## Early draft priorities

- Take one reliable damage plan and one reliable block plan before anything speculative.
- Frontloaded damage (works turn 1 with no setup) is worth more early than scaling that
  only pays off several turns in.
- Don't take defensive payoff cards before the deck can reliably survive long enough to
  use them.
- Respect Vulnerable-generating cards highly — Ironclad's identity leans on Vulnerable
  support plus solid fundamentals rather than exotic combos.

## Common mistakes

- Relying on Burning Blood's end-of-combat heal to cover a structurally weak deck for
  too long — it heals after the fight, not during it.
- Taking too many expensive attacks, producing clunky, energy-starved turns.
- Drafting defensive payoff cards before the deck can survive to use them.

## Build paths

See `builds/` for the three main directions:
- `vulnerable.md` — apply Vulnerable and cash it in with strong attacks; the most
  approachable line, and the one the starter deck (Bash) already leans toward.
- `body-slam.md` — stack Block, convert it to damage; the most forgiving build for a
  rough draft.
- `exhaust.md` — thin the deck deliberately and trigger Exhaust-synergy payoffs; the
  highest skill ceiling of the three.

All three blend well together — Ironclad doesn't need a single dedicated finisher to
win, largely because its archetypes support rather than compete with each other.
