# Silent — Overview

**Core loop:** Sly payoffs, discard flow, and Poison pressure.
**Starter relics:** Ring of the Snake (draw 2 additional cards at the start of each
combat) or Ring of the Drake (draw 2 additional cards on the first 3 turns), depending
on starting configuration.

## Playstyle summary

Silent trades Ironclad's forgiveness for higher per-turn agency: better draw quality,
cleaner sequencing, and more room to pivot deep into a run. She has less margin for
error — a stalled turn feels much worse on Silent than on Ironclad — but the ceiling is
higher for players willing to draft for hand quality first.

## Early draft priorities

- Draft for hand quality first: draw, discard, and low-friction cards that keep the
  deck moving. Silent feels bad the moment a turn stalls.
- Respect defense more than flashy payoffs early — a good Silent deck usually wins by
  reaching its strong turn safely, not by having the strongest single turn on paper.
- Stay open between Discard-Sly and Poison until one plan clearly has better support;
  Silent can afford to stay flexible longer than most characters, but only as long as
  draw keeps both halves of a hybrid plan showing up on time.
- Favor upgrades that make the *next* hand better (draw, discard fixing) over upgrades
  that just make the current hand look stronger.

## Common mistakes

- Drafting payoff cards before the engine pieces (draw/discard/Shiv generation) exist.
- Taking clever, high-ceiling lines over stable ones, ending up with hands that do
  nothing on a bad draw.
- Trying to support both Discard-Sly and Poison indefinitely instead of committing to
  whichever lane is actually winning fights.

## Build paths

See `builds/` for the three main directions:
- `sly.md` — discard-triggered free plays; widely considered the strongest single
  archetype in the game, and the backbone most Silent decks build around.
- `poison.md` — damage that keeps ticking over longer fights.
- `shiv.md` — volume damage from zero-cost Shivs.

Sly plays well with both Poison and Shivs, so it's rarely a standalone choice — most
strong Silent decks run Sly underneath whichever of the other two is winning fights.
