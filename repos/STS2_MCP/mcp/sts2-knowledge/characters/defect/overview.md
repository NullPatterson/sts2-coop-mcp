# Defect — Overview

**Core loop:** Channeling, Evoking, and Focus scaling.
**Starter relic:** Cracked Core (Channel 1 Lightning at combat start) or Infused Core
(Channel 3 Lightning at combat start), depending on starting configuration.

## Playstyle summary

Defect wins fights by setting up a board (Orbs) rather than forcing damage every single
turn — once Orbs are established, the class keeps producing value even on turns where
the hand itself isn't doing much. It plays cleaner than Necrobinder despite also being
system-heavy, because Orb state is easier to track than Necrobinder's multiple
resources.

## Early draft priorities

- Decide quickly whether the deck is surviving through Frost, racing with direct
  damage, or building toward a later burst kill — Defect's three build directions
  don't blend as easily as Ironclad's or Silent's.
- Value stable Orb generation and cycling above cute one-off payoffs.
- Get one real defensive answer online early — Defect improves a lot once the fight
  stops threatening you every single turn.

## Common mistakes

- Drafting scaling cards as if the Orb engine already exists.
- Building around Dark Orb burst too early without enough stall/defense to protect the
  setup turns.
- Ignoring card flow/cycling and blaming "bad orb timing" for a deck that simply
  doesn't draw well enough to keep Orbs channeled.

## Build paths

See `builds/` for the two main directions:
- `orbs.md` — the default, safest plan: build enough Orbs that their passive effects
  alone carry the fight.
- `claw.md` — an alternate win condition built around the Claw card's self-scaling
  damage; strongest early-to-mid run, weaker at high Ascension.

A third archetype, Status (playing into status-card downsides for a payoff), exists but
drops off sharply at higher Ascension levels and isn't detailed here as a result —
treat it as a curiosity rather than a build to commit to.
